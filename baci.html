      What am I looking at?

      This map shows the Constraints Index: where easy access to books matters most for Connecticut's children. Every place gets a need score built from four measures. The higher the score, the more need. It sits beside our book access index, which counts the places to find books that a neighborhood can reach.

      The four measures count equally: the share of adults without a high school diploma, the share of adults who speak limited English, the Grade 3 reading results of the local school district, and the share of people living below 150% of the poverty line.

      The score describes places, not the families or schools in them. A high score means a neighborhood faces more of these pressures at once. It says nothing about any one family, child or school.

      Places drawn with diagonal lines have no Grade 3 reading score, so their need score is based on the other three measures. Gray places have no score, usually because few or no children live there.
